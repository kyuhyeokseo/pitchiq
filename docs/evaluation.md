# Evaluation

All results here use synthetic data generated by this project. They show that the analysis behaves as designed on the project's own synthetic set-ups. They are not evidence that it works on real football (see [Caveats](#caveats)).

Raw reports are in `evaluation/reports/`. Every holdout version has its own report directory.

## Set-ups

Six defensive set-ups, each with two variants: one story where the defence leaves something exposed, and a counter-example where it should not be claimed. The labels below are the ones the reports use. The letters are design labels; this build has no set-up F.

| Set-up | Exposure expected | Counter-example (no exposure expected) |
|---|---|---|
| A | `A:main`: high man-oriented press with a high back line; the striker runs into the space behind | `A:cover`: the same press with an extra covering defender |
| B | `B:main`: high press with a deep back line; a receiver stands between the lines | `B:compact`: a compact block that drops together |
| C | `C:main`: lane-oriented press; central routes screened, a wide outlet and a free receiver open | `C:support`: central numbers, but no free man |
| D | `D:escape`: four players press a goalkeeper, back three and double pivot; the ball escapes through the goalkeeper to the free centre-back | `D:main`: the same trap holds, and the long kick is contested |
| E | `E:step`: passive screen; a central midfielder steps out to the carrier and the man behind him comes free | `E:hold`: the screen holds, and nobody comes free |
| G | `G:main`: back-pass trigger; the back line steps up together, the striker drops with it, stays onside and gets in behind | `G:offside`: the striker stays where he was and ends up beyond the line, so space behind is not claimed for him |

In D, the variant named `main` is the trap, which is the counter-example.

## How the holdout works

- `make evaluate` runs `evaluation/holdout_v2.py` with `evaluation/holdout_v2.yaml`. It generates 12 clips for each of the 12 variants, analyses them causally like a replay, and writes `report.md` and `report.json`.
- A rerun overwrites the report of the version named in the YAML file, so a new run needs a new version and new seeds.
- Seeds and reaction/spacing combinations do not overlap with the demo clips or with the seeds looked at while tuning. Reaction lags, marking cushions, speeds, pass timings and start spots are drawn outside the development settings.
- The runner is the only code that combines the generator's private truth with the analyzer's output. Nothing flows back into the analytics.
- The threshold file (`backend/app/analytics/analytics_config.yaml`, sha256 prefix `2da83f9366f08d6c`) is identical in all six holdout reports. As the reports list them, the changes between versions were to the generator, to two story definitions (C in 0.2, D in 0.6), and one analyzer bug fix (0.4).
- Definitions (from the reports):
  - **Story:** the scenario's designed situation, from its start marker to the last ball action (private truth).
  - **Matching:** same defending team, an accepted analytics family, IoU >= 0.3, and the best single episode.
  - **Designed precision:** the claims found in the matched episode that the scenario was built to produce (or lists as acceptable), divided by all restricted/exposed claims found there.
  - **Wrong emphasis:** a claim the counter-example was built to suppress, supported in any episode of the defending team that overlaps the story. Counted on the 72 counter-example clips.
  - **Abstention:** exposure was expected, but the story stayed partial or unmatched. Counted on the 72 clips where exposure was expected.
  - **Outcome agrees:** the matched episode reports "exploited in synthetic play" exactly when the designed exposure was used.

## Version notes

### 0.1 and 0.2

- 0.1 was run with settings fixed beforehand. Seed 5000 was run once as a smoke test of the runner, and nothing changed before the full run.
- Its failures were diagnosed on separate validation seeds (6000-6011). That led to two changes for 0.2:
  - C's lane screens are now set on the centre-back before he receives. This removed the validation wrong-emphasis cases.
  - The evaluation now accepts the C restriction under either label, central routes screened or short options screened, when markers are near the ball. This is a change of definition, not of the analysis.
- 0.2 then ran once on new seeds.
- Still failing in 0.2:
  - C support claims a free receiver in 2/12 clips, with large reaction lags or wide cushions, so the target of 0 is not met.
  - A main abstains in 2/12, where cover is judged in time.
  - In one G main clip the runner never met the onside trigger, so the long ball was never played.

### 0.3

0.3 measures generator 0.3, which removes most of the players who used to stand frozen off the ball.
- Off-ball slots follow the ball by slopes measured on real play (IDSSE), wander around their spot, keep their spacing and do not stop dead after a run.
- It was developed on seed 7 and seeds 100-119 only, then ran once on new seeds.
- Over all 12 variants (seeds 7 and 100-119), the share of player windows frozen for 4 s while the ball travels 15 m fell from 13.1% to 4.2%. Real play: 1.1%.
- Compared with 0.2, matching and precision held. E step's exposure recall fell (8 of 12 complete), and C support's wrong emphasis rose from 2 to 4 of 12.

### 0.4

0.4 measures generator 0.4, which loosens A's man-marking of all ten outfield players and fixes 0.3's regressions.
- Those regressions were diagnosed on seeds 7 and 100-119 and on validation seeds 6000-6011 only.
- A's markers stay tight near the ball and drop off a man far from it. In real play the nearest defender is a median 4.1 m away near the ball and 8.6 m beyond 45 m.
- The share of A's attackers with a defender within 2.5 m fell from 0.50 to 0.27. Real play: mean 0.16, 90th percentile 0.30.
- E's free man waits at his height before moving into the space, so E step is complete again (12/12).
- The analyzer no longer stops on an outcome revision that comes after a turnover. A new test covers this.
- Episodes outside the designed story rose from 5.87 (0.2) to 7.77 per clip-minute. Players now move with the ball, so more side situations are recognised.

### 0.5

0.5 measures generator 0.4.1, run once on new seeds.
- The browser walkthrough found that D's trap counted 3 v 5 instead of 4 v 5, so D's double pivot now holds its height.
- The same walkthrough found that overlapping episodes covered each other on the timeline. They are now stacked on separate rows.
- Across the two new seed sets for 0.4.x (0.4 and 0.5), matching was 143 and 142 and precision 0.98, close to 0.2.
- The open weak spots were C support's wrong emphasis (1 and 3 of 12) and D main's restriction recall (0.92 and 0.75; it was 12/12 with the 0.2 generator on validation seeds).

### 0.6

0.6 measures generator 0.5, run once on new seeds. Its changes were diagnosed on validation seeds 6000-6011 and on seeds 7 and 100-119 only.
- **No offside releases.** A pass to a runner is never released offside.
  - The old trigger window (1.4 m) was narrower than the distance a runner closes on a stepping-up line within one frame (about 2 m).
  - So in 2 of 21 A main seeds, the long ball went at the deadline with the runner 1.4-1.8 m offside.
  - The trigger now allows for the line's speed.
- **Balls into space keep rolling.** A ball played into space rolls on, slowing, until someone reaches it. Before, it stopped where it landed, and the runner "received" it from up to 2 m away (39 of 643 receptions, all long balls in A and G).
- **C support's free receiver.**
  - The analyzer's free receiver used to be the far centre-back, left 13-20 m from anyone for a square ball. The far winger now steps into that lane, a common way to cut off the switch, so "no free man" is true in play.
  - Validation wrong emphasis went from 1/12 to 0/12.
  - The one 0.6 case (seed 11004) is a different moment: a midfielder free inside after the ball went wide, in an episode that began inside the story window.
- **D main's restriction label.**
  - The analyzer names the trap by whichever label it sees first on the carrier: short options screened when the markers screen before the presser's lane cut is set.
  - The story now accepts either label, as C has since 0.2. This is a change of definition, not of the analysis.
  - Restricted recall rising from 0.75 to 1.00 partly reflects it.
- **Fewer standing players.** C's story players shift their feet where they wait, and the carrier keeps the ball moving.
  - Standing players (path under 2 m in 4 s) in C fell from 5.1-6.0% to 1.4-1.8%.
  - Over all variants they fell from 1.8% to 1.3%. Real play: 0.4%.
- **Tried and dropped:** an analyzer gate requiring the free receiver to be ahead of the ball. In C main the striker stands in the carrier's inside-forward lane, so this press only ever leaves a square option free. The gate removed the designed free man too.

### Known analysis limitation, from validation

When bypassed pressers drop into the midfield line, the line grouping can abstain for a moment. B's between-the-lines receiver is then reported in a parallel free-receiver story instead of the B story.

## Caveats

### How the generator and analysis were developed

- The family-A clip and its counter-example come from one seeded behaviour generator. The scenario parameters were tuned while the analytics thresholds were being written. Getting the expected claims on these clips is therefore not evidence of real-world validity.
- D, E and G were designed on 2026-10-21 and tuned on seed 7 in the same way. Their 20/20 seed results show the generator is consistent, not that the analytics would generalise.
- The demo seed was chosen among early seeds for a clear long-ball outcome.

### Situations the analysis reports in every clip

- While a goalkeeper has the ball in D, four pressers against five plus the goalkeeper always leave a central centre-back free. The analysis says so (a complete trade-off) in both D clips.
- The same happens in the G offside clip once the ball goes back to the goalkeeper (18/20 seeds).

### Model assumptions

- The cover model has no momentum. A back line that is still stepping up is assumed to turn and run at 7 m/s at once. In G the striker therefore runs into the channel between centre-back and full back, where the cover is late even without momentum.
- Lane access, pressure, cover and isolation are fixed-speed geometric heuristics (14 m/s ball, 7 m/s defender, 0.2 s reaction). None of them is a pass-success or goal probability.

### Generator 0.2 realism rules (2026-10-21)

These rules were added after a by-eye review of every clip:
- After a long ball, every player who was holding a slot shifts with the play: the passing team moves up to support, and the other team moves back.
- A player left more than 35 m behind the ball by his own team moves up.
- A defender the ball has gone past by 15 m recovers goal-side.
- A clip ends 5 s after its last ball action instead of standing still.
- A ball-winner no longer runs to his own goal line.

The same review re-tuned A and C and moved the goalkeepers:
- **A:** the centre-back now marks the striker 4-5 m goal-side (it was 2.6-3.4 m). The striker beats him with a diagonal run into the channel between centre-back and full back. In the counter-example, the spare defender follows that channel.
- **C:** the back line now holds about 50 m from its goal behind the lane press (it was about 30 m behind its midfield).
- **Goalkeepers in C, D and E** behind a high line now stand about 11 m off their line.
